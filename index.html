How it works

Two axes. One answer.

Gear wears out on a calendar and in the water, and the two rarely agree. Dive Tend tracks both and surfaces whichever falls due first, so every item carries one plain status.

Ready

Inside its interval on both counts. Nothing to do but dive it.

Due soon

Approaching on one axis or the other. Time to book the shop, not to cancel the trip.

Overdue

Past its interval, with the days counted so you know how far past — not just that it is.

No schedule

Tracked but not scheduled. Honest about what it does not know, rather than guessing.

Calendar intervalMonths

The usual annual or biennial service, counted from the last one you logged — not from the day you entered the item.

Dive countDives

A regulator used on 40 of your 142 dives is due on 40. Dive Tend counts per item, not per diver.

Manufacturer figurePublished

Where a brand publishes its own interval, that is the starting number — and it says so, so you know where it came from.

Your own overrideYours

Dive cold, dive often, or follow your technician’s advice instead. Any interval can be set per item and stays set.

What it isn’t

Dive Tend is a record-keeping tool. It tracks what you tell it, it never invents a service interval, and it does not inspect your equipment. A reminder from it is a reminder — not a certification. Whether your gear is fit to dive is between you, your manufacturer and a qualified technician.
